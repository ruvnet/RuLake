# ruLake Datalake Layer — A Deep Introduction

## TL;DR

ADR-155 ratifies ruLake as a **vector execution cache with deterministic compression and federated refill** — a single-sentence positioning that says the cache hit path is the entire product surface, RaBitQ 1-bit codes plus a SHAKE-256 witness make cross-process cache sharing safe without comparing payloads, and federation is the *refill mechanism* on miss rather than the product shape. M1 shipped the `BackendAdapter` trait, `LocalBackend`, `FsBackend`, the witness-addressed cache with cross-backend dedup, rayon-parallel fan-out with adaptive per-shard rerank, and the bundle sidecar protocol; M2 added `gcs-backend` and `ipfs-backend`; M3 added the `rvdna-backend` and `ruqu-backend` substrates plus their MCP companions. The crate is published to crates.io as `rulake@2.2.0` with a measured 1.02× tax on `LocalBackend` cache hits versus direct `RabitqPlusIndex::search`.

## Introduction

The RVF ecosystem at `vendor/ruvector/` had been shipping the right primitives for years: append-only segment model with a tail manifest (RVF Four Laws), progressive HNSW indexing (Layer A/B/C), temperature-tiered quantization, RaBitQ 1-bit rotation-based quantization (`crates/ruvector-rabitq`, 100% recall@10 at 957 QPS single-thread on n=100k D=128 rerank×20), witness chains (SHAKE-256, Ed25519, ML-DSA-65 PQ), federation primitives (PII stripping, differential privacy, RDP accounting, FedAvg), an HTTP/SSE streaming server. What was missing was the layer that turned those primitives into something an enterprise customer could *adopt* — a thing they could put in front of the data they already had, in the warehouse they already used, with the governance they already enforced.

The first cut of this ADR proposed one deep per-lake integration (BigQuery Tier-1 via external tables and remote functions) and treated the others as Tier-2 follow-ups. That shape works but multiplies the per-backend engineering linearly: each new backend gets its own governance, lineage, and auth story; the RaBitQ compression story lives N times instead of once; users with vectors *spread across* BigQuery and S3 still cannot federate. The reframe — and the reason ADR-155 v2 exists — was that the measured intermediary tax on `LocalBackend` is 1.02× direct RaBitQ. The abstraction is not a bottleneck. We can afford to put orchestration, governance, and routing on top, and the per-lake integration becomes a *push-down optimisation inside the adapter*, not a new product shape.

The v3 reframe (2026-04-22) sharpened this further. The five-axis decision matrix in `docs/research/ruLake/06-positioning.md` (Latency, Simplicity, Governance, Adoption friction, Differentiation) gives cache-first 4 of 5 axes; federation-first wins only on Governance, and even there cache-first scores 4/5 because the cache is the choke point governance lives at. So ruLake stopped being "a federation engine that happens to cache" and became "a cache that happens to federate on miss" — a small change in framing that produces a much smaller crate, a much narrower contract, and a much sharper acceptance test (M1.5: ≥95% of queries return exact top-k without touching the backend).

The reason this matters now is that the RaBitQ compression number had landed and held. 21× compression, 100% recall@10, 957 QPS single-thread, 3.13× over exact flat. Compress once in the cache, serve from any backend — that is the leverage that makes the cache-first stance coherent. The bundle sidecar (`table.rulake.json`, 300 bytes, `RuLakeBundle` at `crates/core/src/bundle.rs:113`) is the portable unit that carries that compression's entropy across processes: `(data_ref, dim, rotation_seed, rerank_factor, generation, rvf_witness, pii_policy, lineage_id, memory_class)` with a SHAKE-256 witness over the witness-affecting fields. Two ruLake instances reading the same bundle from different backends share the cache pointer because the witness is the cache-key anchor — the cross-backend dedup story.

What ADR-155 is *not* doing is also load-bearing. It is not building a vector database (ruLake does not own storage; customers who want a managed vector DB stay on Pinecone / Weaviate / Milvus / LanceDB). It is not replacing BigQuery or Snowflake (these are backends to ruLake, not competitors). It is not promising sub-millisecond latency (cache-hit targets ≤2 ms p99; federated cold path is network-bound). It is not GDPR-compliant out of the box (v1 supports phase-1 logical delete with 30-day phase-2 backend delete; crypto-shredding is v2). It is not a SQL dialect (queries are structured RVF wire — vector + filter predicates — not ANSI SQL; we do not try to reimplement Trino).

## The decision in detail

What got picked. **The `BackendAdapter` trait is the contract** (`crates/core/src/backend.rs:110`-area). Four required methods (`id`, `list_collections`, `pull_vectors`, `generation`) plus an optional `current_bundle` override. Adapters ship as sub-crates so customers with proprietary lakes can implement their own without forking ruLake. **The cache is RaBitQ-native.** Pulled vectors are immediately compressed into 1-bit RaBitQ codes via `ruvector_rabitq::RabitqPlusIndex`. Hot queries answer from the cache (957 QPS, 21× compression); cold queries pull from the backend and prime the cache. Cache coherence rides per-backend manifest-generation numbers. **Governance is a single choke point** between the wire and the planner — RBAC, column masking, lineage, GDPR phase-1 delete, PII classification, and audit log all live there, so adding a backend does not multiply governance work. **BigQuery-native compute is a push-down optimisation inside the `BigQueryBackend`**, not a new product shape. **RVF is the lingua franca both upstream and downstream.** **The portable unit is the bundle sidecar `table.rulake.json`, not the UDF.** The `Generation` is an opaque union (`Num(u64)` for mtimes/versions; `Opaque(String)` for UUIDs and hashes) so Iceberg/Snowflake tokens fit (`crates/core/src/bundle.rs:54`-`98`).

What got rejected. Plug-in-per-lake (the first cut) — multiplies per-backend work and leaves cross-backend federation users without a story. Standalone vector-DB with Parquet import — competes head-on with Pinecone/Weaviate/Milvus/LanceDB without a clear 10× moat. Pure Iceberg table-format extension — real but slow (Iceberg spec evolution is measured in years; kept as a v2 contribution upstream). Trino/Presto connector — Trino assumes SQL and a vector ANN query does not fit the SQL shape cleanly without UDFs; kept as v2. JVM intermediary in Java/Scala — duplicates the hot path in a slower runtime. RVF-purely-inside-a-customer-notebook — works for single-machine single-user; the intermediary unlocks multi-user, multi-backend, governance. Push-through-only (no cache) — simpler coherence but throughput gated by the slowest backend; kept as a mode flag for customers who cannot tolerate cache staleness.

The load-bearing trade-offs. **Cache coherence is a real problem.** Backend updates do not notify ruLake by default. Mitigations are per-backend: Parquet uses file-mtime + filename hash; Iceberg uses snapshot id on the table manifest; BigQuery uses `INFORMATION_SCHEMA.TABLE_STORAGE.last_modified_time`; Delta uses `_delta_log` transaction version; Snowflake uses `SYSTEM$CLUSTERING_INFORMATION` / change streams. Customers with strict consistency requirements run in push-through-only mode and accept the QPS hit. **Latency hop.** Even on cache-hit the RVF-wire round trip adds 1–5 ms over direct library use; customers who call RaBitQ in-process stay in-process. **Owns a new surface** — the planner, the router, the cache eviction policy, the per-backend coherence protocols. **BigQuery's own native Vector Search competes** for pure-BQ customers; ruLake's value is cross-backend + governance + RaBitQ determinism, not single-backend feature parity. **The crate-shape discipline** is preserved by ADR-001: no root workspace, sibling crates only, no `*.workspace = true`, so the IPFS dependencies do not bleed into every consumer of `rulake` core.

## Capabilities

**Federated search across heterogeneous backends.** `RuLake::search_federated(targets, query, k)` (`crates/core/src/lake.rs:521`) is a rayon parallel walk over `(backend, collection)` pairs with adaptive per-shard rerank floored at 5 (`crates/core/src/lake.rs:504`, `:533`). Pre-adaptive, a K-shard federated search paid K× the rerank cost; post-adaptive, the total pre-merge rerank budget is roughly constant in K. Measured: 4-shard concurrent federation went from 0.60× → 0.98× the single-shard QPS, with recall@10 ≥ 0.85 on clustered D=128 n=5k (test `adaptive_per_shard_rerank_preserves_recall`). The over-request formula `k' = k + ceil(sqrt(k * ln(S)))` (`crates/core/src/lake.rs:584`-`590`) closes the data-skew gap that Weaviate / Elasticsearch leave with naive k-per-shard fan-out — at k=10 S=4 this gives k'=13, at k=10 S=16 k'=16.

**Cross-backend cache dedup via witness.** Two backends with identical underlying data produce the same `rvf_witness` because the witness recipe is purely a function of `(data_ref, dim, rotation_seed, rerank_factor, generation_with_variant_tag)`. `crates/core/src/cache.rs::install_prebuilt_interned` shares the compressed entry. Test `two_backends_share_cache_when_witness_matches` is the gate.

**Bundle publish + refresh** as the cache sidecar protocol. `RuLake::publish_bundle(key, dir)` writes `table.rulake.json` to a directory atomically (temp + rename); `RuLake::refresh_from_bundle_dir(key, dir)` reads it back and returns `RefreshResult::{UpToDate, Invalidated, BundleMissing}`. A corrupt or tampered sidecar surfaces as `InvalidParameter` via the witness-verification path so a poisoned publish cannot silently invalidate the cache. The sidecar daemon itself is ~10 lines of user code — the protocol is the primitive, not a daemon.

**Warm restart via persisted index.** `RuLake::save_cache_to_dir(key, dir)` snapshots a primed cache entry to `dir/index.rbpx` plus the companion sidecar; `RuLake::warm_from_dir(key, dir)` reads them back, witness-verifies the sidecar, dim/rerank-checks the index, and installs into the cache via the interned path — no backend round-trip, no RaBitQ recompression. Two operators warming the same bundle share one compressed entry.

**Worked example.** A research collaboration has 100k vectors in a Parquet file on GCS, 10k vectors in an in-memory `LocalBackend` for tests, and 50k vectors served by an `FsBackend` on a local SSD. The operator registers all three backends under one `RuLake` instance. A federated query (`search_federated([("gcs", "papers"), ("local", "fixtures"), ("fs", "snapshot")], q, 10)`) fans out in parallel: each shard primes its cache on miss, each shard's RaBitQ search runs concurrently, the top-13 per shard (over-requested for 3-shard skew) merges by score, the global top-10 returns. Per-backend `cache_stats_by_backend()` reports hit rate, prime time, and witness count per backend; per-collection `cache_stats_by_collection()` is one level finer. The 95% hit-rate gate is measurable directly from `CacheStats::hit_rate()` without external tracing.

## Trust & correctness contract

The trust anchor is the per-bundle witness as cache key. Five things matter, all citable.

**The witness recipe is bytes-only.** `compute_witness` (`crates/core/src/bundle.rs:362`-area) is SHAKE-256(32) over a domain-prefixed, length-prefixed, variant-tagged concatenation of `(data_ref, dim, rotation_seed, rerank_factor, generation)`. The domain prefix `rulake-bundle-witness-v1|` makes the digest non-overlapping with any other SHAKE consumer; every variable-length input is preceded by its length in little-endian u64 so `"a|b"` and `"ab|"` cannot collide; the `Generation` enum carries a 1-byte variant tag at `crates/core/src/bundle.rs:73`-`98` (`0x00` for `Num`, `0x01` for `Opaque`) so `Num(7)` and `Opaque("\x07\0\0\0\0\0\0\0")` cannot produce the same digest — a real bug closed by the 2026-04-23 security audit, bumped as `format_version = 2` at `crates/core/src/bundle.rs:163`.

**The witness is the cache-key anchor.** `crates/core/src/cache.rs::install_prebuilt_interned` keys by witness; `RuLake::cache_witness_of(key)` (`crates/core/src/lake.rs:144`) returns the witness pinned by a `(backend, collection)` pointer; `RuLake::cache_refcount_of(witness)` (`crates/core/src/lake.rs:157`) counts the external pointers resolving to it; `RuLake::cache_entry_count()` (`crates/core/src/lake.rs:151`) is smaller than the pointer count when multiple backends share a witness.

**The bundle is verified at every parse.** `RuLakeBundle::from_json` (`crates/core/src/bundle.rs:215`) enforces `MAX_JSON_BYTES = 64 * 1024`, per-field length caps, and rejects `format_version > 2`. `RuLakeBundle::verify_witness` (`crates/core/src/bundle.rs:191`) recomputes the digest from the parsed fields and returns the equality. `RuLake::refresh_from_bundle_dir` rejects a tampered sidecar with `InvalidParameter` so a corrupt publish does not silently poison the cache (`crates/core/src/lake.rs:228`-`258`).

**Construction is the only witness-emitting path.** `RuLakeBundle::new` (`crates/core/src/bundle.rs:166`) computes the witness during construction; the bundle is never observed without its anchor. Backends that synthesise bundles in `current_bundle` always go through `RuLakeBundle::new` (the `IpfsBackend::current_bundle` fast path at `crates/ipfs-backend/src/backend.rs:354` is the canonical example).

**The variant-tag fix and the format-version bump are explicit.** `RuLakeBundle::FORMAT_VERSION = 2` at `crates/core/src/bundle.rs:163` is the on-disk version; v1 bundles will not match v2 witnesses, which is intended — the variant-tag fix is a breaking change to the witness, and operators with persisted v1 bundles re-prime the cache. The doc comment at `crates/core/src/bundle.rs:158`-`162` is the canonical record.

## Reference implementation status

What shipped, in the order it landed.

- **M1 (weeks 1–2, shipped, measured)** — the `rulake` crate scaffold with `BackendAdapter` trait, `LocalBackend` + `FsBackend`, RaBitQ-cache glue, witness-addressed cache with cross-backend sharing, LRU eviction over unpinned entries, rayon parallel fan-out with adaptive per-shard rerank, bundle sidecar protocol, and hit-rate / prime-time instrumentation. 28 tests passing. Acceptance numbers from `BENCHMARK.md`: 1.02× intermediary tax on `LocalBackend` at n=100k, 2-shard 1.97× / 4-shard 3.86× rayon prime-time speedups, recall@10 > 90% on clustered D=128 rerank×20 single-shard, Send+Sync under contention (8 threads × 50 queries).
- **M1.5 (acceptance test for the cache-first reframe)** — `CacheStats::hit_rate()` (`Option<f64>`) and `avg_prime_ms()` plus raw `total_prime_ms` / `last_prime_ms` exposed. Serving processes can emit hit_rate directly as the headline cache-first KPI; the 95% gate is measurable without external tracing.
- **M2 storage adapters (shipped)** — `crates/gcs-backend/` (Parquet on GCS via `object_store`, commit `c706dc6`), `crates/ipfs-backend/` v0.1 (witness-anchored bundle distribution by CIDv1, with R-IPFS-1 hard-refuse on CID mismatch in commit `56b497b`).
- **M3 substrate adapters (shipped 2026-04-27)** — `crates/rvdna-backend/` v0.0.1 (genomic hot-tier, ADR-007) and `crates/ruqu-backend/` v0.0.1 (StateVector quantum execution, ADR-008), both with criterion benches and focused security review on file at `docs/research/{benchmarks,security}/`.
- **Companion MCP servers** — `crates/mcp-server/` v0.10 (general; commit `56b497b` added symmetric mutation-handler audit + R-MCP-1 fix), `crates/mcp-rvdna/` v0.0.1 (5 tools, witness-drift refusal), `crates/mcp-ruqu/` v0.0.1 (5 tools, qubit-cap + circuit-id guards).
- **Crate published to crates.io** as `rulake@2.2.0` (commit `f04eb95`); manifest at `Cargo.toml:1`-`36`.

What stayed roadmapped at M4+. Iceberg / Delta / BigQuery adapters. RBAC / PII / lineage / audit choke point (M4 in the original plan). Crypto-shredding for GDPR (v2). JVM client for the wire protocol (enterprise customer ask, deferred). A Trino/Presto connector. Push-down negotiation policy (when does the planner prefer native push-down over the cache? Probably when cache hit-rate < 20% for the collection — needs a policy, not a constant).

## Composition with the rest of ruLake

The crate is the trunk that every other ruLake artefact branches from.

The `BackendAdapter` trait at `crates/core/src/backend.rs:110` is what every backend implements — `LocalBackend` (in-tree), `FsBackend` (in-tree, the `ruvec1` binary format at `crates/core/src/fs_backend.rs`), `GcsParquetBackend` (sibling crate `crates/gcs-backend/`), `IpfsBackend` (sibling crate `crates/ipfs-backend/`), `RvdnaT0Backend` (sibling crate `crates/rvdna-backend/`, ADR-007), `RuquStateVectorBackend` (sibling crate `crates/ruqu-backend/`, ADR-008). Each is `cargo install`-able independently and registered in the operator's serving binary via `Arc<dyn BackendAdapter>`.

The bundle (`crates/core/src/bundle.rs:113`) is what travels between processes. Its witness anchors the cache (`crates/core/src/cache.rs`); its `data_ref` points at the underlying byte stream (a backend's URI); its `generation` is the coherence token. The witness is what makes cross-deployment cache sharing safe; the `generation` is what makes coherence checkable; the `lineage_id` is what makes the audit pipeline join across federation hops.

The lake (`crates/core/src/lake.rs:48`) is the public entry point. It owns the backend registry, the cache, and the consistency posture. `search_one` (`crates/core/src/lake.rs:475`) is the single-target path; `search_federated` (`crates/core/src/lake.rs:521`) is the multi-target path; `publish_bundle` / `refresh_from_bundle_dir` is the sidecar protocol; `save_cache_to_dir` / `warm_from_dir` is the warm-restart path; `cache_stats_by_backend` / `cache_stats_by_collection` is the operator-visible KPI surface (`crates/core/src/lake.rs:127`, `:136`).

The MCP server (`crates/mcp-server/`, ADR-004) wraps the lake's surface in eight tools (3 Read tier, 2 Publish, 3 Admin) and three resources (`rulake://stats`, `rulake://bundle/{backend}/{collection}`, `rulake://audit/tail`). The Console (`ui/`, ADR-006) wraps the MCP wire in a tri-mode browser UI. The substrate adapters (`crates/rvdna-backend/`, `crates/ruqu-backend/`) ride the same trait. The `mcp-rvdna` and `mcp-ruqu` companions ride the same audit pipeline (`crates/mcp-server/src/audit.rs::AuditEntry`) with disjoint `RVDNA_*` / `RUQU_*` / `RULAKE_*` code prefixes.

A substrate, in this framing, is anything that satisfies the trait and produces witnesses through the canonical recipe. ADR-155 is the contract that makes substrate-shaped contributions land without a federation rewrite, a cache-layer fork, or an audit-pipeline duplication.

## Open questions

Push-down negotiation is the largest M2+ unknown — when a backend supports native push-down (BQ Vector Search, Snowflake Cortex), at what point does the planner prefer it over the cache? Probably when cache hit-rate < 20% for the collection, but the policy is unwritten. JVM client for the wire protocol is spec'd as v2 but the enterprise-pipeline customer will ask in week one. Cost accounting — when the planner pushes down to BQ, whose BQ credits are burned? Needs a customer-facing cost-attribution story. Remote-backend tax measurement — `BENCHMARK.md`'s 1.02× tax on `LocalBackend` is the floor; M2 needs to measure the real tax on a Parquet-on-GCS prime and document the p50/p99 numbers the BQ UDF path can expect. Strategic positioning unknowns — invisible-infrastructure-vs-user-facing-query-layer, strict-freshness-vs-10×-throughput as a product knob. The ADR is honest about each as deliberately not "engineering open questions" but answers that shape what ruLake *is*.
